# Research summaries (R-<topic>-<n>)

Every rule is a paraphrase with its source URL. Cite as `research-summaries.md R-FORM-2`. Rules marked (excerpt) rest on a search-result excerpt of the page, not a full read. Material Design 3 is not included: its pages render client-side and could not be fetched, so no M3 rule is claimed.

## Buttons vs links
- **R-BTN-1** Use links to move between pages and buttons for commands that change something. Keep buttons for the primary command people scan for; show minor or secondary commands as links so the primary one stands out. Button labels stay short, about four words or fewer. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/command-links/
- **R-BTN-2** Make clickable things look clickable: consistent link colour that contrasts with body text, buttons with clear shape and border, no heading styled like a button, icon plus text label, and image plus caption sharing one click area. Flat styling needs extra signals (border, colour, size) to compensate for lost depth. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/clickable-elements/
- **R-BTN-3** Design every state: enabled, hover, focus, pressed, disabled, loading. Give feedback quickly after a press, show focus with an outline that does not depend on colour alone, and show loading inside the button. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/button-states-communicate-interaction/

## CTA labels and placement
- **R-CTA-1** A label is a promise. Make it specific, sincere (the next screen delivers exactly that), understandable on its own, and brief. Front-load the key word. A long specific label beats a short vague one. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/better-link-labels/
- **R-CTA-2** Avoid "Learn more" and "Click here". Name the outcome or topic, in the audience's goal language ("Get a quote for a 5-page site"). https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/
- **R-CTA-3** State what you do in one plain tagline near the top; do not assume visitors know the brand. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/
- **R-CTA-4** Put the highest-priority content and the main CTA near the top: eye tracking found about 57% of viewing time above the fold and about 74% within the first two screenfuls. Repeat the CTA further down, where a decision gets made. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/
- **R-CTA-5** Do not emphasise everything. If every block shouts, nothing is primary; keep one dominant action per screen. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/

## Touch and thumb reach
- **R-TOUCH-1** Aim for roughly 1 cm by 1 cm physical size for tap targets; primary CTAs go larger. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/
- **R-TOUCH-2** Size comes first, spacing second: about 2 mm between targets, but spacing cannot rescue a target that is too small. Make targets larger for people on the move, children, older users. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/
- Thumb-zone placement (bottom third reachable one-handed) has no NN/g page I could verify; treat it as a heuristic, not a citable rule.

## Cards
- **R-CARD-1** A card bundles related pieces (image, title, short summary, action) as a teaser to fuller content. Signal that the whole card is clickable with a border, contrasting background or light shadow. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/cards-component/
- **R-CARD-2** Do not use cards when people must search or compare items; a list or table scans and compares better. For case studies, keep the same fields in the same order on every card. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/cards-component/
- **R-CARD-3** Decorative images that alternate sides (zigzag) slow scanning because the eye stumbles over them; align decorative images on one side. Informational images (real screenshots, results) work in either layout. Do not add filler images. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/zigzag-page-layout/

## Accordions and FAQ
- **R-ACC-1** Use a caret as the expand icon; avoid plus signs, right arrows or custom glyphs. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/accordion-icons/
- **R-ACC-2** The label and the icon must do the same thing (no split button where the text navigates and the icon expands). People tap either one. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/accordion-icons/
- **R-ACC-3** On mobile, accordions give an overview and shorten long pages. Watch two pitfalls: an expanded section scrolling to the top can feel like a new page, and very long answers force scrolling back up. Use sticky headers or a clear way to close, and avoid the word "Collapse". https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-accordions/
- **R-ACC-4** Collapsing content shortens the page but makes people choose among headings, which raises interaction cost; write question headings that are recognisable at a glance. (excerpt) https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-accordions/

## Forms
- **R-FORM-1** Put a visible label above each field on mobile. Placeholders are not labels: they vanish, strain memory, block error checking and have contrast and screen-reader problems. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders/
- **R-FORM-2** Keep forms short: remove every field you do not need, use one column, group related fields, and mark the few optional ones. State format requirements up front. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/
- **R-FORM-3** Validate inline once a person finishes a field, not when they first enter it. Confirm success on complex fields. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/errors-forms-design-guidelines/
- **R-FORM-4** Show each error next to its field in visible text, not in a tooltip or modal; use more than colour (icon, outline, weight), and keep what the user typed so they can fix it. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/errors-forms-design-guidelines/
- **R-FORM-5** A summary at the top is not enough; pair it with field-level messages. If someone hits the same error three or more times, offer extra help (contact link). https://www.nngroup.com/articles/errors-forms-design-guidelines/
- **R-FORM-6** Skip Reset and Clear buttons; accidental data loss outweighs any benefit. Match input type and size to the expected answer; for two or three choices prefer radio buttons to a dropdown. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/

## Testimonials and social proof
- **R-PROOF-1** Social proof works because people follow other people; low numbers can backfire (a participant judged an item unpopular from about 1,000 shares). Show a strong figure or use qualitative proof instead of a weak count. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/social-proof-ux/
- **R-PROOF-2** Test that visitors notice the proof and that it helps rather than clutters; do not assume it converts. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/social-proof-ux/
- **R-PROOF-3** Credibility of a testimonial rises with who wrote it: name, job title and organisation. Quotes that begin with a doubt and end in confidence read as believable. (excerpt) https://www.nngroup.com/articles/social-proof-ux/

## Section rhythm on mobile
- **R-RHY-1** Do not leave big empty bands: minimalist pages can look finished ("false floor") so people stop scrolling. Show a cut-off element or heading at each fold edge, and keep density moderate. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/
- **R-RHY-2** Write for scanners: short taglines, clear hierarchy, no dense text blocks, minimal pop-ups and animation. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/
- **R-RHY-3** For long mobile pages use an overview near the top, accordions, sticky navigation and a back-to-top control. (excerpt) https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-accordions/

## Before and after comparisons
- **R-BA-1** Choose images that carry information (real results, screenshots) over decoration, and give them descriptive alt text. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/imagery-in-visual-design/
- **R-BA-2** Do not rely on drag-only sliders: give two labelled images side by side or step buttons (derived from WCAG 2.5.7, https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).
- No NN/g page on before/after sliders was found; the annotation advice seen in a search excerpt could not be traced to a readable page and is not claimed.
